Itan Kitchen

A fictional modern Nigerian restaurant in Manchester. This page is my response to the restaurant digital transformation case study, written as the restaurant’s Chief Technology Officer.

Business context

Itan Kitchen serves modern Nigerian food, from smoky jollof to suya-spiced vegetables. It takes bookings only by phone, prints its menu on paper, has no way to collect feedback and has little social media presence.

This website has four goals:

  • Customer engagement: promotions, a loyalty scheme and social media links.
  • Operational efficiency: an online form that replaces most booking phone calls.
  • Customer experience: a menu with dietary labels that works well on phones.
  • Online communication and promotion: feedback and enquiries in the same form.

Privacy and security

We ask only for the details needed to answer you, and we ask for your agreement before anything is sent. Your details go only in the email you choose to send; this prototype has no database.

This site sets no tracking cookies. The reminder at the bottom of the page is there so visitors know how cookies would be handled if analytics were added later. Under UK GDPR, you can ask us to see, correct or delete your data at any time by emailing us.

Please don’t put sensitive information, such as payment details or health records, in your message. The form checks your input in the browser before it creates the email.

Design choices

I used the warm brown and beige palette to suggest food and hospitality, and kept the layout simple so the menu and form are easy to read on a phone. Headings and labels use a bold sans-serif for clarity; the body text uses a serif for comfortable reading.

The form uses JavaScript to show only the fields that match the request, to check input, and to build the email with a mailto: link. A real system would send the message from a server so it works without the visitor’s email app; mailto: is a suitable choice for a prototype with no database. Semantic headings, labelled fields, visible keyboard focus, descriptive page titles and alt text support accessibility and basic SEO.
